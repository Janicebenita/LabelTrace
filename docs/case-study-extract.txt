Theme: Intelligent Medical Device Labeling Automation Platform
Design innovative, scalable, and compliant solutions that transform the end-to-end medical device labeling lifecycle by 
leveraging Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), Generative AI, Robotics Process Automation (RPA), Computer Vision, 
Smart Workflows, and Cloud Technologies.
The challenge encourages participants to rethink traditional labeling operations and create future-ready solutions that accelerate 
regulatory compliance, reduce human effort, minimize errors, and enhance patient safety.
Key Takeaways: Rethink traditional medical device labeling operations and create future-ready solutions that accelerate 
regulatory compliance, reduce human effort and labeling design & implementation cycle time, minimize errors, and enhance 
patient safety.
Medical devices are sold in highly regulated markets where the information supplied with the device is not simply packaging 
artwork. The label, packaging information, markings, symbols, instructions for use (IFU), warnings, precautions, Unique Device 
Identification (UDI) and related information communicate critical information about the device and its safe use.
Medical device labeling is one of the most critical regulatory and patient-facing components of a medical device lifecycle.
A single label may contain (Not limited to):
◦ Product identification
◦ Unique Device Identification (UDI)
◦ Regulatory symbols
◦ Country-specific requirements
◦ Multi-language content
◦ Safety warnings
◦ Sterilization information
◦ Traceability data
◦ Manufacturing details
Today many organizations still rely heavily on (Not limited to):
◦ Labeling Specialized Resources (e.g. people, tools, change management system etc.)
◦ Traditional Labeling Design practices & standards operating procedures (SOPs)
◦ Manual content updates
◦ Spreadsheet-based tracking and monitoring
◦ Multiple approval cycles and delayed approvals 
◦ Human verification and validation
◦ Repetitive artwork generation
◦ Fragmented systems (e.g. multiple PLMs, SAP etc.)

Sustainability Impact
1. Time-Consuming Manual Process- Experts manually review specifications, regulations, labels, gaps, redlines, revisions and 
change documentation. This creates high cycle time, repetitive work, expert dependency and scalability challenges.
2. Frequent Regulatory Updates - Teams manually monitor new regulations, revised standards, new/revised symbols, UDI 
requirements and market-specific requirements. The key risk is missing affected labels.
3. Symbol Management - Experts manually determine applicable symbols, correct versions, placement, associated text and 
market applicability. Errors can cause compliance issues, rework and production delays.
4. Multiple Sources of Truth - Relevant information may reside in PLM, ERP, requirements documents, regulatory databases, 
specifications, spreadsheets, artwork files, previous labels and quality systems.
5. Expertise Dependency - Labeling requires product, engineering, regulatory, symbols, UDI, quality, artwork and 
manufacturing knowledge, creating bottlenecks and training challenges.
6. Manual Redlining - Experts manually compare current and required labels and create redlines. Computer vision and 
document intelligence could automate difference detection.
7. Repetitive Review - The same checks—product number, manufacturer, symbols, UDI, dates, warnings, language and 
formatting—are repeatedly performed and are candidates for rules-based automation.
8. Traceability - Teams need to answer why each label element exists and trace it to product data, a requirement, source 
document, approval and change order.
9. Variant Explosion - Multiple products, configurations, markets and languages can create thousands of potential label 
combinations.
10.Human Error - Manual copy/paste, outdated symbols, incorrect identifiers, missing information, inconsistent terminology, 
wrong revisions and wrong market information can introduce risk.
